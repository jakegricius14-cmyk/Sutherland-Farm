The garlic

Siberian Hardneck.

One variety, chosen because it thrives in Alberta winters and tastes the way garlic should.

At a glance

What makes it special.

Siberian is a “Marbled Purple Stripe” hardneck: a cold-hardy garlic with purple-streaked skins and a few large cloves in every bulb.

TypeHardneck, Marbled Purple Stripe
Cloves per bulbUsually about 5 to 7 large cloves
FlavourRich and medium-hot, mellowing in storage
PeelingEasy. Big cloves, loose skins
Keeps forAbout 6 months when stored well
Getting the most from it

Store it, cook it, grow it.

How to store it

  • Keep it somewhere cool, dry and dark, like a pantry.
  • Let it breathe: a basket, mesh or paper bag. Not plastic.
  • Don’t put whole bulbs in the fridge. They can sprout or go soft.
  • Break a bulb only when you’re ready to use it.

How to cook it

  • Roast whole bulbs until soft and sweet, then spread on bread.
  • Crush or chop and wait a few minutes before cooking for the fullest flavour.
  • Add it near the end of cooking if you want more bite.
  • Big cloves make it quick to prep a lot at once.

Want to grow some?

  • Plant single cloves in late September or October.
  • Pointy end up, about 5 cm deep and 15 cm apart.
  • Cover with straw for the winter.
  • Pick the biggest cloves: big cloves grow big bulbs.
Hardneck vs. store-bought

Why it tastes different.

Most garlic in the grocery store is softneck garlic grown for long shipping and shelf life. It has many small cloves and a milder taste.

Hardneck garlic, like our Siberian, grows a stiff centre stalk and fewer, larger cloves with a richer flavour. It’s harder to grow on a large scale, which is why you mostly find it at local farms.
