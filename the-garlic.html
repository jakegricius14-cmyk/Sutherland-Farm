The garlic

Four varieties.
One farm.

Mild to hot

Meet the four.

01

Persian Star

Purple Stripe hardneck

Mild

Rich and gentle with a mild zing. Flavourful without taking over the dish.

Brought to North America from a bazaar in Uzbekistan in the late 1980s. Its red-tipped, streaked cloves sit in a star shape, which is where the name comes from.

Cloves
8 to 12
Keeps
5 to 7 months

Best with

  • Lemon
  • Yogurt
  • Mint
  • Lamb
  • Rice

Try it in: Tzatziki, hummus, salad dressings, lamb stew, rice pilaf.

02

Siberian

Marbled Purple Stripe hardneck

Medium

Robust and well-rounded. Turns creamy when cooked and mellows the longer it’s stored.

Said to have travelled from Russia to Alaska with fishermen who traded for it. Big bulbs with a handful of fat, easy-to-peel cloves.

Cloves
5 to 7
Keeps
6 to 7 months

Best with

  • Potatoes
  • Butter
  • Chicken
  • Root veg
  • Cream

Try it in: Roasted whole, mashed potatoes, soups, roast chicken, everyday cooking.

03

Italian Purple

Purple-striped hardneck

Bold

Hot and strong when raw. Roasting turns it sweet, nutty and spreadable.

A beautiful purple-streaked garlic with plump cloves. A favourite in Mediterranean kitchens.

Cloves
8 to 12
Keeps
A few months

Best with

  • Tomatoes
  • Basil
  • Olive oil
  • Eggplant
  • Parmesan

Try it in: Pasta sauce, pizza, garlic bread, bruschetta, roasted vegetables.

04

Russian Red

Hardneck, often grouped with Rocamboles

Hot

Rich, robust and spicy raw. Rounder and nutty when cooked, but it keeps its kick.

Named for its burgundy-mottled skins. Said to have come to the Pacific Northwest with Russian immigrants in the early 1900s, and a classic across Canada.

Cloves
6 to 12
Keeps
5 to 6 months

Best with

  • Beef
  • Ginger
  • Soy sauce
  • Chili
  • Mushrooms

Try it in: Stir-fries, curries, chili, steak marinades, garlic butter.

Why local

Not grocery-store garlic.

  • Grown a few minutes away, not shipped from overseas.
  • Hardneck garlic: fewer, bigger cloves and richer flavour.
  • Cold-hardy varieties, suited to Alberta winters.
Good to know

Store it, cook it, grow it.

Store

  • Cool, dry and dark.
  • In a basket or paper bag, not plastic.
  • Not in the fridge.

Cook

  • Chop, then wait a few minutes before cooking.
  • Add it late for more bite, early for mellow.
  • Never let it burn: it turns bitter.

Grow

  • Plant cloves in late Sept or October.
  • Pointy end up, 5 cm deep, 15 cm apart.
  • Cover with straw for winter.
